⌘T goes where you meant, open or not

The ⌘T panel has a name now: Navigator. In 0.4 it found the tabs you already had open. Now it covers the rest of what ⌘T is for. Type a few letters of a site and the page you visit most that starts with them is the top row, so you goes to youtube.com. Anything that isn't an address is a web search. Your history is ranked by how often and how recently you visited each page, across every profile, and fuzzy matches against open tabs sit below that.

The search uses the browser's own engine, read from the profile (in Safari, its own “search the web”). To use one engine in every browser, pick it in “Searches with” under the ⌘T switch in the rules window, or add a search template line to the rules file.

  • The row under the cursor says where ↩ goes: “Go to youtube.com”, or “Search google.com”.
  • Nothing is selected until you type or press ↓, so ↩ on an empty panel still opens a new tab the way it always did.
  • Rows appear at once from the last list Navigator read, and the list is refreshed behind them.
  • Navigator hides when you switch to another app or click outside it.
  • Pasting a paragraph gives you one search: line breaks become spaces, and the field stays one line that scrolls sideways.
  • Type settings to open the BrowserRouter rules window without reaching for the mouse.
  • Titles and addresses now line up on one line, and shortcut keys sit in square keycaps.
  • The rules file still calls it tabswitcher, so existing rules keep working.
